D13–D15 are accepted go-public decisions (2026-11-14, product owner): GPL-3.0-only with DCO-only contributions, a free core with free official binaries and paid support as the first revenue experiment, and a canonical-identity continuity statement; their operational gates are tracked in the [support matrix](support-matrix.md) and issues #78–#82, and the working launch plan is kept outside the repository by decision (2026-11-14, see the [docs index](README.md)). Scope changes belong in the [product plan](product-plan.md); wire/schema details belong in [contracts](data-and-api-contracts.md).

## D01: Personal local archive

Choose a single-user desktop archive with optional remote ingestion and AI. Ordinary reading/search works offline. A unified inbox with sending, shared team permissions, and a hosted knowledge service are deferred because they change the product and trust model. Success is finding and preserving an original exchange, not maximizing connector count.

## D02: Retain the current implementation stack

Keep Tauri, Rust, SQLite/FTS5, TypeScript and Vite. Separate feature controllers, IPC, state, view models, rendering and styles as the UI grows. A frontend framework rewrite or backend service would add migration cost without addressing the demonstrated correctness gaps. Revisit only with measured complexity/performance evidence.

## D03: Separate source, conversation, message, and occurrence identity

Use opaque durable source/conversation IDs and preserve source-specific keys as aliases. Store folder/import occurrences separately from messages. Build email context from reply evidence, never folder/subject alone. Keep conflicting revisions and explicitly unresolved legacy records. Canonical v2 tables avoid the old source-string uniqueness constraint; preserve old IDs and retain legacy tables for recovery during this release. The cost is migration complexity and temporary disk duplication; silent identity loss is less acceptable.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): implemented as M1.1–M1.4 and verified (canonical tables with frozen legacy snapshot, connector-scoped identities with semantic revision hashes, provider conversation keys with a one-hop email reply graph, multi-folder occurrences, explicit unresolved records, and the safe-migration snapshot/rollback path). Discord remains on legacy display grouping; user linking of legacy namespaces is designed but not implemented. Details and evidence: [contracts](data-and-api-contracts.md#identity-and-deduplication), [M1 record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

## D04: Serialized writes and independent readers

Use one writer worker, two read connections, WAL and bounded producer queues. Network/parsing waits happen outside transactions. This directly addresses the current network-held shared mutex without adding a database server or unbounded pool. Checkpoint and result persistence are one transaction. Concurrency limits and initial batch sizes are owned by [Architecture](architecture.md#jobs-and-database-access), with measured validation before release.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): implemented as M2.1–M2.3 and verified — the `DbPool` writer worker + two read connections with per-connection initialization and bounded write/job queues; job bodies hold no connection (network waits never inside the writer, a lock, or a transaction, proven by stalled-network tests); imports and IMAP fetch batches commit atomically with their checkpoints and issue/failed-item ledgers. Measured validation landed with M2.4 (real-path paginated-search baselines, concurrent-ingest measurements; [BASELINE.md](../tests/perf/BASELINE.md)). Deviations recorded honestly: all background jobs serialize on one runner thread (stricter than proposed), the busy timeout is 5 s, and job records are per-process rather than persisted rows. Details: [Architecture: jobs](architecture.md#jobs-and-database-access), [M2 record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

## D05: Preserve originals and available media locally

Copy source originals and available regular media into SHA-256-addressed storage; keep metadata/relationships in SQLite. Do not rely on external export paths remaining present. Shared bytes are deduplicated, while message revisions/provenance remain separate. Missing/unmatched files stay explicit. Complete available media preservation increases release work and disk requirements; it was expressly selected instead of a text-only release.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): the object-store half is implemented as M4.1 and verified — SHA-256-addressed `media/` storage with the stage→promote→reference lifecycle (no half-written object ever visible, no dangling reference reachable), byte-exact copies with the original name/kind/size kept on every reference, content dedup across names, messages, and imports, traversal/symlink/capacity-fenced ZIP and directory picks, Telegram/WhatsApp media extraction with missing files counted in the import summary, reimport backfill of media links, and store-vs-archive reconciliation (`media_reconcile`). Still open under this decision: mail MIME attachments and preserved raw message bytes, unlinked source files, per-message missing/failed states (M4.2) — so “missing files stay explicit” is today a summary count, not yet per-reference rows — plus safe open/export surfaces (M4.3) and backup/restore of object bytes (M4.4). Signal and Discord attachments remain `raw_json` records only. Details: [Architecture: originals and media](architecture.md#originals-media-and-object-lifecycle), [contracts: media and capacity](data-and-api-contracts.md#media-and-capacity).*

*Amendment (2026-11-16, attachment-content indexing dependency — [#171](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/171)):* the umbrella attachment-search feature moves attachment-content indexing off the [product plan's](product-plan.md#deferred-work) deferred list into staged implementation, starting with PDF text layers. **Trigger:** #171 was accepted and split into reviewable parts; part 1a needs a PDF text extractor, and the engine/licensing choice must be recorded before any dependency enters the tree (the umbrella's own rule: distribution choices documented before native dependencies). **Decision:** text-layer extraction uses the pure-Rust `pdf-extract` crate (MIT) over `lopdf` (MIT, `default-features = false`) — no native C libraries, both GPL-3.0-compatible as dependencies of this GPL-3.0-only repository. Extraction is local-only (no document bytes or derived text leave the process), bounded by explicit size/page budgets, returns explicit corrupt/encrypted/unsupported/over-budget results, and the derived text stays separate data about preserved objects, whose bytes are never modified. OCR for scanned PDFs and images stays deferred until its part decides the engine, licensing, and language packs; this amendment covers text-layer extraction only. Final sign-off of the dependency happens at pull-request review of part 1a. **Alternatives rejected:** a hand-rolled extractor directly on `lopdf` (font/encoding handling is the hard half and `pdf-extract` already carries it); native bindings such as Poppler or MuPDF (C dependencies and heavier per-OS distribution review, against the staged plan); OCR at extraction time (engine not yet decided). **Compatibility:** no schema, job, search, settings, or frontend change in this slice; nothing user-visible changes until the later parts of #171 land. **Validation:** targeted `cargo test -p vault-core --lib pdf_text` over committed synthetic fixtures (the existing media fixture `plan.pdf`, a new Unicode text PDF, and a password-protected PDF). **Migration:** none — no stored data changes.

## D06: Consistent, verified backup and staged restore

Use a versioned ZIP64 backup with a SQLite snapshot, objects, and checksum manifest. Pause writes during backup for the first release to simplify consistency while retaining read access. Validate restore in staging and retain the previous vault through a journaled switch. A live database-file copy and in-place unverified restore are rejected because WAL and interrupted writes can invalidate recovery.

## D07: OS protection with explicit encryption limits

Rely on OS account/disk protection and owner-only permissions where supported. Store credentials in the OS keychain with visible session-memory fallback. Database, objects and backups are not encrypted by the app. App-managed encryption and recovery-key UX are deferred by user choice; no branding or UI text may imply they already exist.

## D08: Archive workspace over competing feature panels

Use source navigation, flat search results, and a reading pane. Setup, activity and maintenance have dedicated views. Keep ranking stable and display human labels before technical metadata. Adapt to narrow windows with back navigation. This preserves familiarity and reading context while avoiding competing Search/Ask/import/server sections on one page. The [UX specification](ux-specification.md) owns breakpoints and keyboard rules.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): the workspace/search halves are implemented as M3.1/M3.2 and recorded — the three-area shell with navigation destinations (All messages / Add source / Activity / Settings) and per-source scope chips, design tokens with light/dark/System themes and the bundled Source Sans 3, wide/medium/compact layouts with a 200%-zoom ≡ compact equivalence, the typed IPC boundary and centralized stale-response guard, and the flat stable v2 ranking (rank/id and instant/id orders, unknown times last) with filter chips, honest unknown-time and unverified-grouping presentation, deep links, and selection/scroll restore. Honest deviations: per-source status text and account pages await M3.4, the import surface keeps its interim pre-preview flow until M3.3, and the v1 render module stays active for unmigrated panels until the M3-exit cleanup. Details and evidence: [UX specification](ux-specification.md#workspace-and-navigation), [M3 progress record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

## D09: AI is optional and payload approval is explicit

AI defaults off regardless of environment keys. Each question has an explicit translation send action; synthesis requires a preview/approval of the exact bounded context. Bind approval to endpoint, configuration and snapshot. Reuse the existing compatible-provider integration and fallback; attachments and arbitrary tools remain outside the pipeline. This adds one deliberate step but makes network disclosure concrete and keeps retrieval useful without AI.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): the consent/citation halves are implemented with M5 and recorded — AI stays off regardless of environment keys, now enforced in the backend: without an explicit, per-session consent a network provider selection is downgraded to a fully local ask before any provider client exists (zero wire requests without consent, proven by mock-endpoint tests), the first network ask of a session shows a per-question disclosure of exactly what would leave, citations `[n]` and the numbered source rows open the original archived message in thread context through the v2 reading pane, and provider failure leaves local results usable. Honest deviations from this decision's text: consent is session-scoped and in-memory only (no persistent switch — resets every app start), the approval is one per-session grant after the first per-question prompt rather than a per-question translation send plus a separate digest-bound approval of the exact preview, and asks do not yet intersect active filters — those remain the M5.1/M5.2 remainder. Details and evidence: [Architecture: optional AI ask layer](architecture.md#optional-ai-ask-layer), [M5 record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

*Amendment (2026-11-16, session-scoped consent is the final release behavior — [#181](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/181)).* **Trigger:** this decision and the M5.1 slice proposed a persisted off-by-default consent switch; the shipped M5 implementation deliberately made consent session-scoped (in-memory only, reset on every app start, never written to storage), and the roadmap held the choice between the two open for a product decision. **Decision:** session-scoped consent is the release behavior; the persisted switch is superseded, not deferred. A consent can never survive an app restart — stricter than the proposed switch, whose persisted grant could stay enabled indefinitely — and "AI defaults off regardless of environment keys" is unchanged, now holding afresh at every app start. **Affected user behavior:** nothing changes; users keep re-consenting once per app session through the first-ask prompt, with the always-visible status line and mid-session Revoke. **Alternatives rejected:** building the persisted off-by-default switch (weaker on disclosure longevity, and it would add a Settings surface and persisted state for no user gain); re-prompting for every ask (noisier than the decided per-session grant). **Compatibility:** no code, schema, settings, or migration change — consent was never persisted, so there is nothing to migrate; the M5.2 digest-bound-approval remainder of this decision is unaffected and stays open. **Validation:** the recorded M5 consent-gate evidence stands unchanged; this is a documentation-only decision, so no suite was rerun (standing docs-only validation decision). Closure recorded in the [product plan's decision record](product-plan.md#ai-consent-is-session-scoped-decided-2026-11-16-181) and the roadmap's M5.1 decision addendum ([implementation roadmap](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking)); the UX, architecture, contracts, and assessment sections carry the supersession notes.

## D10: Read-only mail ingestion and opt-in scheduling

Keep IMAP read-only and archive content independent of remote deletion. Default to manual sync; optionally enqueue one run every 15 minutes while open, without missed-run accumulation. Retain Gmail app-password support; OAuth is deferred. Source disconnection stops access/removes secrets but does not delete local history. This is an archive, not a bidirectional mailbox replica.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): implemented as M2.3/M2.4 and verified — read-only `EXAMINE` access with atomic fetch-batch commits and the durable failed-item ledger; the opt-in scheduler persists its schedule in the vault, submits each round as an ordinary `imap_sync_all` job, never overlaps rounds, and collapses sleep/resume/offline gaps into one catch-up round (missed time cannot queue). One deviation grew beyond the original text: the Gmail browser OAuth2 flow (XOAUTH2) shipped alongside app passwords, so the “OAuth deferred” clause is superseded — both paths are supported, chosen in the UI. Details: [Architecture: IMAP correctness](architecture.md#imap-correctness), [M2 record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

*Amendment (2026-11-11): opt-in background keep-alive (M7). **Trigger:** the 2026-11-06 product review names source freshness as the next investment, and the new-mail notifications (#59) were practically unreachable — nothing syncs while the app is closed, and a focused open window suppresses the notify path, so the existing push loop could almost never fire. **Decision:** the app may keep running with its window closed, strictly opt-in. Two new default-off Settings options — “keep running in the background” (window close hides to a tray icon instead of exiting) and “start automatically at login” (OS autostart registration, applied when Settings are saved) — plus a single-instance guard: a second launch focuses the existing window instead of opening a second pool over the same vault. The tray menu offers Show Mailchat Vault, Sync now (an ordinary `imap_sync_all` job through the existing job engine — identical to the manual round), and Quit. **Unchanged:** the scheduler, coordinator, jobs, and watch layers are untouched (they already re-arm and round correctly); ingestion stays read-only; nothing about *what* runs changed — only *whether the process lives between window closes*, and only when the user asks. **Alternatives rejected:** a separate headless daemon/service binary — two processes over one SQLite vault (write contention, recovery-journal interplay), its own credential/keyring story, and double the packaging surface, for marginal gain over tray keep-alive; it is a documented non-goal, not deferred work. **Compatibility:** both toggles default off; settings files written before the fields existed load with them false (serde backfill), and closing the window behaves exactly as before (process exits) unless the user turned keep-alive on. macOS keeps its convention-friendly shape — the toggle simply gives Linux and Windows the same close-without-exit ability; on any platform where the tray icon cannot actually be built (no appindicator library on Linux, or any tray build failure), keep-alive degrades to today's behavior: closing exits (with a loud log warning), never a hidden unreachable process. A desktop that accepts the StatusNotifier icon but renders no status area (e.g. GNOME without the AppIndicator extension) is a known limitation: the icon registers invisibly, and recovery is a second launch, which the single-instance guard focuses. **Validation:** settings round-trip/backfill unit tests (vault-core + registry + frontend payload), an e2e spec asserting the process stays alive with the window hidden after a close request (Xvfb-friendly: process liveness + window visibility read out of webview — the X server for the map state, the app's /proc entry for liveness), and the standard suites. **Migration:** none — the settings file format version stays 1; no schema change. Details: [Product plan](product-plan.md#functional-requirements) (P10), [M7 record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

## D11: Three-platform release with honest evidence

Target Linux x64, Windows x64 and macOS arm64/x64 for the first polished release. Native compilation, packaging, credentials, upgrade and restore evidence are mandatory per target. Keep the current Linux automated E2E and add repeatable native checks where drivers differ. Signing/notarization are publication dependencies; unavailable credentials or hardware do not count as passing evidence. Automatic updates remain deferred.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): the build/packaging/smoke-automation halves are implemented and recorded — the three-platform release workflow (Linux AppImage/deb on `ubuntu-22.04`, universal macOS dmg on `macos-latest`, Windows msi/NSIS on `windows-latest`; single-owner draft release, artifacts attached per platform), desktop/MIME integration for Linux with the automated deb/AppImage smoke suites on clean 22.04/24.04 containers (green at landing), and the four-phase Windows installer smoke (fresh install with the archive created in the app-data path, uninstall never touching the archive, in-place upgrade from the frozen v0.1.0 fixture with identical content digests) — driven green against disposable fake installers, with no real-runner dispatch on record. Honest evidence per this decision's own rule: nothing is signed or notarized (an external dependency — the exact wiring is documented, the accounts do not exist); the published `v0.1.0` shipped with both release runs recorded as failures and no smoke gate executed against its artifacts at publish time, and without checksums — corrected on 2026-11-06 by the release-fix change (SHA256SUMS attached retroactively and verified against the server-side digests; the Linux suites rerun against the published artifacts, failing only the pre-#47 MIME checks); macOS had no automated smoke beyond the native build and the manual checklist until `scripts/release/smoke-macos.sh` landed 2026-11-06 (no run recorded); no push-triggered CI run on `main` is recorded after the M3.1-era commit. Per this decision these do not count as passing release evidence — the completed public release remains unbuilt. The new-mail notifications (#59) landed inside this decision's platform surface (cross-platform OS permission integration, silent degradation) and are recorded in the [M6 progress record](implementation-roadmap.md#completion-tracking).*

*Amendment (2026-11-15, release execution route — [#80](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/80)):* the per-OS authorized execution route is decided. While GitHub-hosted runners cannot start (account payments/spending limit since 2026-11-14 — the route (c) re-enable of PR #85 stands but cannot execute), the authorized route for every OS is the **local native build**: Linux via the docker-pinned `build-linux.sh` + clean-container `smoke-linux.sh` (available today), macOS/Windows on real hosts via their one-command matrix columns (host availability is [#81](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/81)), assembled by `prepare-release.sh` and published through `gh release` behind the same gates; the hosted workflows become the default again per release once a run actually executes on the tag — YAML presence alone stays non-evidence under this decision. A release may mix routes per OS provided each OS's assets carry that OS's executed gates. **Provenance is planned separately:** GitHub artifact attestations require an executing workflow (Actions billing restored — the CI-debt record is [#102](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/102) and the [interim per-PR protocol](testing.md#interim-per-pr-verification-while-ci-is-down)) and an eligible repository plan (public repository, or an eligible plan for a private one — the public move itself waits on [#79](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/79)); only then is the attestation step wired into the release build jobs, gated on `gh attestation verify`. Until then `SHA256SUMS` (from `prepare-release.sh`, checked against GitHub's server-side asset digests) remains the integrity baseline and no release claims attestation-backed provenance. Detail: [release.md → Release execution route](release.md#release-execution-route-decided-2026-11-15-80).

## D12: Dates retain provenance and uncertainty

Store original timestamp text separately from a resolved UTC instant and confidence. Require user confirmation of naive import timezone/date order. Preserve legacy assumptions without silently reinterpreting them; date filters use workspace-local day boundaries. The additional metadata and occasional import question are preferable to presenting a false exact time as evidence.

*Amendment (2026-11-05): implemented as M1.3 and verified (per-message instant + original text + zone + confidence; DST-aware exporter-local resolution with fold/gap handling; migration 7 resolves legacy blind-Z stamps without reinterpreting recorded text; v2 search interprets day bounds in a validated IANA zone). The user-confirmation half (`user_confirmed`) awaits the M3.3 import flow; legacy values keep `legacy_assumed` until then. Details: [time semantics](data-and-api-contracts.md#time-semantics).*

## D13: GPL-3.0-only license with DCO-only contributions

License the repository under GPL-3.0-only (accepted 2026-11-14). The `-only` variant is deliberate: contributions stay bound to the GPL-3.0 terms exactly, without automatically accepting unknown future license versions; as sole author, the project owner retains relicensing rights over their own work. Apache-2.0 was weighed as the permissive alternative and kept rejected for now — reciprocal distribution of the core matters more than maximal adoption (go-public review S1/S9). Contributions require DCO sign-off only (`Signed-off-by`); the project deliberately takes **no** proprietary relicensing grant — DCO does not provide one, and no custom short-form grant may be invented. If a proprietary dual-license product ever becomes a strategic requirement, a separately reviewed CLA or assignment must be adopted *before* accepting contributions to the relevant code. Trademarks, logos, documentation and screenshots are licensed separately from code, without blanket brand exclusions that would break redistribution and packaging. The LICENSE file and `Cargo.toml` declaration land with this decision; the dependency/asset inventory and public-history scan before any visibility change remain open ([#79](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/79)).

## D14: Free core and free official binaries; paid services first

Import, local search, reading, available-media access, export and backup/restore stay free — including ordinary business use of the GPL-3.0-only core — and **official core binaries stay free too**: the trusted download path is never a purchase barrier. This supersedes the earlier paid-binaries fallback. Revenue, when tested, starts with paid onboarding/import/setup assistance and bounded support (the €79–149 session in the launch plan's monetization experiment — kept outside the repository — is a test hypothesis, not a price commitment), not with a new edition or account system. No ads, no sale of user data, no compulsory account, no paid AI dependency, no managed AI relay; paid convenience must not weaken data portability or move private content to a service by default. Expanding beyond paid support requires the plan's gate: at least three unrelated paying customers with successful outcomes and positive contribution after labor.

## D15: Canonical identity and continuity statement

Use one canonical repository/download page as the identity from the moment anything is public; an owned domain follows when the name, owner and renewal responsibility are settled — renewal reminders and account recovery matter more than a fixed multi-year term, and a domain is not a prerequisite to discovery. Publish a continuity statement limited to what can actually be delivered: locally usable data in standard formats, documented backup/export limitations, source and build instructions under GPL-3.0-only, and a last-release/recovery guide if maintenance ends. No promise of indefinite support, and ordinary exports are not forensic evidence.

## Changing a decision

Add a dated amendment explaining the trigger, affected user behavior, alternatives, compatibility, validation and migration impact. Mark the old decision superseded and link its replacement. Update the owning specification and roadmap slices together; do not leave conflicting instructions for implementers.
