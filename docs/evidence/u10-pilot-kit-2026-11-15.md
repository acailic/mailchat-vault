# U10 pilot kit — supported 30-day pilot runner materials (2026-11-15)

Status: **prepared; not started**. This kit is the runnable substrate for [GP06 / issue #96](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/96), the U10 gate every public step waits on. It contains no participants, no observations and no results — those require human recruitment, participant agreement and 30 elapsed days. Preconditions: [GP03 (#93)](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/93) and [GP04 (#94)](https://github.com/acailic/mailchat-vault/issues/94) were **open** when this kit was prepared; the pilot may not enroll anyone until both have passed. The gate rule itself lives in the [gate record](u10-pilot-gate-2026-11-15.md).

Sources: the protocol sections of the [user research playbook](../internal/user-research-playbook.md) (recruit-by-behavior screener, thirty-day pilot, recording templates) and the thresholds and decision rules of the [user success plan](../internal/user-success-plan.md) (U10 row, staged gates, decision rules). Where this kit and those documents differ, they win; fix the kit.

## What this kit is and is not

In: the recruitment screener, the day-0/7/30 schedule with the support arrangement, the participant tracking log, and the gate-readout template with its decision rubric.

Out: recruiting or contacting participants (an authorized user action), running sessions, and writing the readout (written only after observation). The kit adds no telemetry, no analytics and no participant-identifying data; it is paper for the human running the study.

## 1. Recruitment screener

Cohort size: **ten private-pilot participants** (playbook, recruit by behavior), drawn from the three recruitment hypotheses — independent professionals, researchers, people preserving an old account. These are **hypotheses to test, not segments to fill**: participants qualify by observed behavior and source availability, never by job title.

Screen with the playbook's discovery questions, asked about the last 90 days:

1. Have you tried to find a past work/project message or attachment and struggled? Describe the last occasion.
2. Which apps/accounts held the possible answer? Was the source still accessible?
3. What did you try, roughly how long did it take, and what happened if you could not find it?
4. Do you have email plus WhatsApp, Telegram or another currently supported export? Can you obtain copies you are comfortable using locally?
5. Which desktop and phone do you use? Where were you when you needed the information?
6. Have you used an archive/export/search tool before? What made you keep or abandon it?

Qualification rules (from the playbook, kept binding here):

- Qualify on a **concrete recent failed or slow retrieval the person can describe (and ideally show)**, plus a supported source and willingness to keep original export copies locally. Liking privacy or AI never qualifies; hypothetical enthusiasm never qualifies.
- Record source availability, operating system, technical comfort and retrieval frequency at screening; carry them into the tracking log.
- Someone needing an unsupported source is useful discovery evidence but is **not** a pilot participant and must not be silently converted into a supported-path success.
- Preservation-only participants (preserving an old account, no recurring retrieval need) enroll as a **separate analysis group** whose outcome is successful independent restore and reopening of a known message/file — not retrieval frequency.
- Exclusions: no supported export obtainable; platform outside the U09-validated pilot platform set; unwillingness to keep original copies or to use an explicitly labeled research build.
- Reuse discovery/usability participants only where useful; record prior exposure in the log and prefer new participants where earlier teaching would bias the result.

Recruitment record (aggregate, non-identifying, this repository may hold it only as counts): recruited / qualified / declined / excluded, with exclusion reason categories. Issue anonymous participant IDs (P01…) at enrollment; store the identity mapping only in the researcher's access-controlled location outside this repository.

## 2. Pilot schedule

Per participant: 30 elapsed days from the day-0 onboarding check. Build: the U09-validated release-candidate artifact for the chosen pilot platform(s), explicitly labeled as a research build, version recorded per participant. Participants keep their original exports/source copies — the research build is never their sole backup.

### Pre-flight (before any day 0)

- [ ] GP03 (#93) and GP04 (#94) have **passed** with recorded evidence; the pilot gate's preconditions are closed.
- [ ] Pilot platform(s) chosen from validated U09 records; labeled build artifact pinned, version and checksum recorded.
- [ ] Screener completed; qualification rules applied; participant agreement (ordinary authorization, voluntary participation, right to stop at any time) recorded outside this repository.
- [ ] Support channel open (see below); anonymous participant ID issued.

### Day 0 — onboarding check (≈60 minutes, participant's own machine)

Observe; assist only after recording the failure; count every intervention.

1. Participant installs the labeled build. Record install time, platform, artifact version.
2. Participant prepares their own source copy (their real export). Record export-preparation time **separately** from app time.
3. Participant imports through the unified picker and reviews the import summary (new/existing/failed, missing files, coverage).
4. First verified retrieval: the participant finds and opens an original **they** care about, and confirms it answers their actual question. A result count or summary alone is not activation.
5. Preserve-and-move check: participant makes a backup and (for preservation-focused participants, as the primary outcome) restores it in a separate test vault and reopens a known message/file. Never overwrite a personal vault for the study.
6. Record: funnel stage reached, assistance count, exact obstacles (labeled summaries), support minutes.

### Days 1–29 — in-use period

- For each **genuine** retrieval opportunity, the participant may voluntarily record a task entry (§3). No-opportunity participants are recorded as such, not as failures.
- Support: one named channel with stated response expectations (suggested: within one business day). Support records timing, counts, failure categories and problem descriptions **in the participant's words**. Support collects **no archive contents**: no vault files, no message bodies, no screenshots of private conversations, no query text, no contact names, no account addresses, no credentials. If diagnosis seems to need content, the participant paraphrases or describes; nothing is sent. Every contact is logged with minutes and a cause category.

### Day 7 — follow-up (≈20–30 minutes)

The playbook's prompts, kept in order:

- "Since the last session, when did you need an old exchange or file?"
- "Which tool did you reach for, and what happened?"
- "What did you expect to be here that was missing or stale?"
- "Did maintaining/exporting the history take more effort than the lookup saved?"
- "If this app disappeared tomorrow, what would you actually do instead?"
- "What would have to work differently before you trusted it with another source?"

Also record: current funnel stage (still active / stalled / withdrew, with reason category), support minutes to date, second-source additions (tracked separately — cross-source value cannot be claimed from single-source activation).

### Day 30 — follow-up (≈30 minutes)

- Repeat the day-7 prompts over the full period.
- Preservation-focused participants: perform the independent restore-and-reopen outcome task.
- Record final stage, genuine-opportunity count, chosen tool per opportunity, and any withdrawal (participants may stop at any time; withdrawers stay in the denominator with their reason).

### After the last day-30 — the readout

The gate owner assembles the readout from the tracking log using §4's template, preserves raw denominators, and applies the decision rubric. The readout is filed against the gate record; until it exists, **no broader promotion happens**, whatever else is green.

## 3. Participant tracking log

Kept in the researcher's access-controlled location outside this repository. Only aggregate findings and synthetic evidence ever enter the repository. No message bodies, author identities, account addresses, query text, credentials or file contents anywhere in the log.

One participant record:

```text
Anonymous participant ID:
Recruitment hypothesis (independent professional / researcher / old-account preserver; preservation-only flagged):
Build / platform / prior exposure:
Source category / source availability at screening / technical comfort / retrieval frequency:
Enrolled (date) / withdrawn (date + reason category):
Funnel stage reached: recruited → installed → source ready → imported → first verified retrieval → activated → genuine later opportunity → useful reuse
Second source added (yes/no, kind):
Support minutes: total / contact count / repeating cause categories
```

One task record (the playbook's template, verbatim):

```text
Participant ID:
Build / platform / prior exposure:
Task ID / source category / source coverage:
Export preparation / installation / import time:
Retrieval time / correct original found:
Assistance count / exact obstacle:
Expected capability versus observed capability:
Current-method comparison:
Participant explanation (summary or consented verbatim quote, labeled):
Researcher interpretation / confidence:
```

Rules: write entries only after observation; a coached completion is recorded as assisted, never converted into an unassisted success; keep raw numerators and denominators; never average away a dropout, a failure or an unusable setup.

## 4. Gate-readout template

One readout per pilot, filed when the last day-30 follow-up is done. The field list is the playbook's gate-readout template, verbatim:

```text
Date / hypothesis / tested build:
Recruited / qualified / completed / withdrew:
Install → source ready → import → verified retrieval → activation counts:
Later opportunities / useful independent reuse / no opportunities:
Task success and failure categories, with raw denominators:
Comparison results and setup effort:
Recovery correctness and comprehension:
Total support time / median / range / repeating causes:
Evidence that contradicts our preferred direction:
Decision: proceed / narrow / one focused iteration / stop expansion
Reason / owner / next hypothesis and pass rule:
```

Decision rubric (the success plan's decision rules, as a checkable list — check every line, cite the log entry for each):

- **Proceed to broader beta** requires all of: first-source and retrieval thresholds passed; restore proven; a meaningful subset returns for real jobs; support problems bounded; users can explain a concrete advantage over alternatives.
- **Narrow** when one segment/source combination succeeds while others fail: improve and describe that specific workflow; stay honest about the remaining formats.
- **Repeat one focused iteration** when need is clear but a single identified onboarding or relevance problem causes failure: state the hypothesis and the pass rule **before** the next cycle begins.
- **Stop expansion (or reconsider reuse/integration)** when qualified users repeatedly prefer current tools or the closest alternative, source preparation overwhelms the benefit, or there is no repeat/preservation value. Do not answer a failed hypothesis with more undirected features.
- **Hard stop regardless of averages:** any reproducible loss or corruption of archived data, or any unintended content transmission, stops expansion.

Thresholds being tested (proposed decision rules from the success plan — not observed results, not benchmarks; report raw counts alongside any percentage):

| Measure | Proposed threshold | Measurement rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return value | ≥6 of 10 activated participants report a genuine retrieval opportunity; ≥4 of those independently choose Mailchat again and verify a useful result | Report both the opportunity-adjusted fraction (e.g. 4/6) **and** the whole-cohort fraction (e.g. 4/10) if those are the actual numbers; a no-opportunity participant is not automatically satisfied |
| Support burden | Median onboarding assistance ≤15 minutes per participant | Record total, median, upper range and repeating causes; a low median must not conceal several unusable setups |
| Preservation group | Successful independent restore and reopening of a known message/file | Separate analysis group; not penalized for having no reason to open an archive every day |
| Funnel | Every stage with raw numerator/denominator and dropout reason | recruited → installed → source ready → imported → first verified retrieval → activated → genuine later opportunity → useful reuse; define activation as one user-owned source imported plus one verified retrieval |

Filing rules: link the readout back to F01–F12 in the [product review](../internal/product-review.md) and U01–U10 in the [success plan](../internal/user-success-plan.md); record contradictions with the preferred direction rather than smoothing them; an interview request, passing engineering test, planned feature or attractive demo is not a completed user outcome.
